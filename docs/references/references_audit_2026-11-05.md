# Checking every SDP_7036 source: what holds, what breaks

Most of the 32 journal references hold up. Authors, venue and year check out for most of them, and most of the "what we took" notes are fair. But a set of errors must be fixed before submission. **NIST SP 800-108r1 was withdrawn on 2 Feb 2024**, so cite SP 800-108r1-upd1. **Volt Boot [3] is a physical attack**: the attacker holds the SRAM rail with a probe. No isolation request is ever issued, so our interlock does not stop it. **Intel's TME-MK spec rev 1.7 says "at least 29b SHA-3 based integrity"** and says nothing about ECC or 28 bits. BadRAM [14] did not "replay past an address-bound MAC". The patent notes have attribution errors. The Freescale and Cadence descriptions are swapped. US9395805 is LSI/Seagate, not Intel. The Altera zeroize-verify patent is US8437200, not US8736299. NXP US11689363 does claim key wrapping, in claims 11-12. Several items are still unverified: [4], [9], [13], the Chypothermia venue, AMD-SB-8018 and the Assertain venue. On novelty, the narrow claim survives. In this sample, **no paper or patent gates power-domain isolation on a verified key erase and proves it with the power manager left unconstrained**. Standby key wrap, wipe on clock stop, debug/test erase, zeroize-verify and power-aware formal checks are all prior art. This was a sample check, not a full freedom-to-operate (FTO) patent search.

## How to read the tables

Each group below has one table. **Ref no.** uses the final numbering [1]-[32] from the 2 Nov reference list. B1-B4 are base papers, C1-C5 are candidates and N1-N2 are the shortlist. Sources found in the 3 Nov handoff review are marked "added 3 Nov". Patents carry their patent number, since they are kept out of the journal list. **Status** is one of three words. *Verified* means a source we could reach confirms it. *Needs fix* means the record has an error. *Unverified* means we could not confirm it. It does not mean the item is wrong.

A few terms are used throughout. **IME** (inline memory encryption) means encrypting data on its way to DRAM. **XTS** is an AES mode built for storage that uses the address as a "tweak", so the same data at different addresses encrypts differently. **Isolation** (iso_en) clamps the outputs of a power domain before it switches off. **The interlock** is our rule that iso_en can only latch once erase_done is set and no valid key remains. **Zeroize** means wipe a key. **Key wrap** means encrypt a key under another key, the **KEK** (key-encryption key). **DFA** (differential fault analysis) recovers keys from faulty outputs. **CWE** is MITRE's list of weakness types. "Project-PDF only" means the detail comes only from your own PDF downloads and was not found online.

Two do not. **Volt Boot is a physical rail-hold attack, so our interlock does not defeat it.** The warm-boot paper [1] is about externally powering a removed DIMM, not passive remanence. The 3 Nov additions are well read. On Borrowed Time is actually *closer* to our idea than the record says.

## What this means for your novelty

The narrow claim survives. In everything checked, papers and 20+ patents, **nothing latches power-domain isolation only after a verified erase with no valid key left, and nothing proves such a rule with the power manager left unconstrained.** That is a "no hit in this sample" result, not a full FTO search. The broad claims do not survive. "Erase keys before power-down" is suggested in Cold Boot [28], Volt Boot [3] and On Borrowed Time. Hardware standby key save is in Intel TME [22] and Intel EP4020181. Wipe on clock stop is On Borrowed Time. Debug/test erase is Apple US8120377 and Freescale US7185249. Zeroize-verify is Altera US8437200 and Marvell US8645716. Power-aware assertion generation is Cadence US7958475 and Mentor US8839164. So write "no prior engine **gates isolation on verified erase**", never "no prior engine erases keys".

Word the CWE-1272 gap narrowly. A safe version reads: *"MITRE lists only manual analysis for CWE-1272 and notes that it could be automated ([MITRE](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/1272.html)). CWEAT places it out of scope. Recent LLM assertion tools cover the related CWE-226 and CWE-1239 but not CWE-1272. We give, to our knowledge, the first formal check that ties the clear to the power-isolation transition under an unconstrained power manager."* Add that applying CWE-1272 at an isolation boundary is your own reading.

The near-misses a reviewer is most likely to raise are these. **Qualcomm US9785211** already makes isolation wait for a ready condition, though the condition is empty buffers. **Apple US9043632** gives the secure block a veto over its own power gating, but without erase. **Altera US8437200** and **Marvell US8645716** verify zeroization. Together, those three make an obviousness argument plausible. That is why the patent track was dropped, and why the paper must lean on the proof and the measured 45 nm cost. **On Borrowed Time** suggests clearing before power gating, without an interlock or proof. **OpenTitan** wipes keys on alerts, and Chypothermia shows that failing when cold. Also expect NXP US11689363 (wrap + flush), Intel TME standby save, Ghosh [8] (event-triggered destruction), SecDDR (new key every power-up), and the self-erasing cells [10] and [11]. Finally, state the threats the interlock cannot cover. Physical rail holding (Volt Boot), undervolt or cold clock freezing (Chypnosis, Chypothermia), aging imprint (UnTrustZone) and replay (Battering RAM) are all outside what a power-manager-driven interlock can stop. Naming them up front makes the narrow claim look deliberate, not lucky.
